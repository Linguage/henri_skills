# Translated articles for public readers

Use this module when translating articles, revising existing translations, or preparing them for public readers. It supplements the project's translation workflow. It does not turn a translation into Henri's original writing or authorize deployment.

## T1 — Source-author fidelity

Preserve the source author's arguments, order, voice, uncertainty, satire, strong judgments, examples, time conditions, and disclosures. Retain source title punctuation and substantive contrast structures even when Henri's original-writing rules would reject them. Do not rewrite an author's commentary as a neutral report, add missing causal relations, remove meaningful repetition, or change numbers to agree with a newer page. Newly authored summaries and translator notes must follow Henri's applicable expression constraints.

## T2 — Public reader and public evidence

A public reader has not participated in collection, translation, or publication. Article provenance and added factual explanations must cite the article's own public HTTP(S) URL or a relevant public primary source. An article's self-link is distinct from another work cited inside it. Use the publisher's or author's verified canonical article URL when available.

Do not cite subscription messages, private mail archives, local documents, collection manifests, audit records, or internal processing paths as public evidence. Keep this provenance in private management records. Remove explanations about receiving messages, extracting text, recovering placeholders, cleaning tracking links, removing ads, and comparing local files when they have no bearing on the reader's understanding.

This requirement concerns provenance and added evidence. Preserve the author's substantive discussion of emails, Gmail, local files, code, and development tools. Deployed images and other site assets are presentation resources; their relative paths are not private evidence citations. Keep localhost examples as code, with a public project/documentation link when needed, rather than presenting them as externally accessible references.

## T3 — Metadata and material boundaries

Verify author, publisher, article title, and original publication date from public sources. Do not substitute receipt, collection, translation, deployment, or update dates for the original publication date. Remove private date-basis and local provenance fields from public metadata; keep collection records outside the public draft. If a date cannot be verified, leave it unknown or follow the target's unknown-date convention without inventing one.

Distinguish the translated article title, its original title, and the encompassing newsletter issue title. A single feature inside a newsletter uses that feature's original title; the issue title may identify its public container. HTML metadata and the visible H1 can differ: inspect the page before declaring a title changed. State actual scope for excerpts, speech selections, and interview-derived articles; do not label an excerpt a full interview or a newsletter introduction the full linked article.

## T4 — Version correspondence

Check whether the current public page materially differs from the text being translated. A public link must not silently imply that stale source text matches an updated page. Resolve demonstrated differences within the authorized scope by translating the relevant current passage, or use a concise, publicly supported version/correction note adjacent to the affected passage. Preserve the source author's original statement when annotating an error; identify the correction as the author's or translator's, as appropriate.

Private receipt dates cannot establish a publicly verifiable historical version. If evidence establishes that a substantive block exists only in a private copy, find a public version or remove that unsupported block with an internal change record. A subscription preview, unavailable page, or title/date mismatch alone does not establish private-only content and does not justify deleting unviewable translated paragraphs. Do not claim a historic version merely because it appears in a mail export. Distinguish original publication from later revisions and do not backdate current performance claims or statistics.

## T5 — Attribution and useful translator notes

Keep necessary terminology, code, arithmetic, scope, and ambiguity notes. Label them as translator additions and place material corrections near the affected sentence, using a stable footnote or concise note. Link the public source supporting the specific explanation. A reference list alone does not repair a misleading statement or establish independent verification.

Keep original author statements, third-party quotations, forwarding introductions, sponsor disclosures, and translator additions distinguishable. An introduction from a forwarder requires its own public source; without one, remove the introduction and correct the publisher attribution. Preserve relevant conflicts/disclosures even if unrelated advertising is omitted. Do not append generic warnings or claim every cited assertion was independently validated.

## T6 — Numbers, technical examples, and ambiguity

Preserve a number's comparison baseline, denominator, units, population, date, and qualifying conditions. If a source is wrong, keep attribution and add a publicly supported correction rather than silently improving the author's argument. Distinguish supplier claims, illustrative charts, predictions, and independent measurements. General population statistics cannot be recast as subgroup results.

Preserve code and command logic. Repair demonstrable transcription or typography errors against the public source with a short note when needed; do not normalize quotes throughout unrelated prose or silently fix a bug also present in the original. An improved runnable example must be separately labeled. Resolve confirmed translation ambiguity using the source and surrounding context, changing only the necessary words.

## T7 — Access and missing references

Distinguish readable public full text, subscription preview, unverified target, and demonstrated dead link. Tool errors, a login footer after visible full text, CAPTCHA, or an open.substack redirect failure do not establish a paywall or a dead article. A publicly addressable paid article remains a public source link; do not bypass access restrictions or claim its complete text has been checked from a preview.

Recover genuinely missing public links, videos, or images only when the target corresponds to the cited item. Use verified DOI or publisher metadata for a missing bibliography target, without claiming to have read the paper. If a target remains unknown, do not fabricate it; retain necessary textual attribution and a narrow reader-relevant limitation only where needed. Keep detailed verification limits in the management report, not a generic processing diary appended to every article.

## T8 — Minimal revision and preservation

Save a review baseline outside public output. Read the affected sentence with its neighbors and paragraph before and after every change. Tie each edit to an identified issue and this module or the user's explicit request. Preserve unaffected text, image captions, formulas, footnotes, code, and attachments. Do not regenerate the entire translation for a local citation or metadata defect.

Use deterministic scripts for metadata mapping, file export, and structural checks; use semantic review for attribution, version correspondence, and translator wording. Re-export after changing drafts and inspect the actual target artifacts: an adapter can reintroduce fields or alter code even when the source is correct.

## Acceptance

- Source and translator voices are distinct; unaffected source text and meaningful disclosures remain intact.
- Public article references use verified public targets or explicitly bounded unresolved references; no private source record serves as evidence.
- Publication metadata matches public evidence; scope, title layers, and material revisions are represented accurately.
- Added corrections preserve source attribution, quantitative baselines, historical dates, and access limitations.
- Every edited sentence and paragraph has been reread in context; images, code, formulas, and footnote targets still work.
- Actual export artifacts have been checked, and remaining limits are recorded without claiming unperformed full-text or factual verification.
